rh is the event horizon, not a photon sphere
The notebook defines rh as the outer Kerr horizon. Unstable circular photon orbits sit at rPro (co-rotating) and rRetro (counter-rotating). The shadow is the capture cross-section on the distant observer’s sky — larger than either photon sphere, and D-shaped when viewed near the equator of a spinning hole. It is drawn on the sky plane, so it follows your viewing inclination but not the hole’s rotation.

Equations (M = 1)

Notebook definitions

rh(χ) = M (1 + √(1 − χ²))

rPro(χ) = 2M (1 + Cos[⅔ ArcCos[−χ]])

rRetro(χ) = 2M (1 + Cos[⅔ ArcCos[χ]])

χ = 0 is Schwarzschild: rh = 2M, rPro = rRetro = 3M, shadow radius = 3√3 M ≈ 5.196 M. χ → 1 is extremal Kerr: rh = rPro = M, rRetro = 4M. Prograde photons hug the horizon; retrograde photons recede.
